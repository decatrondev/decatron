BOT DESCRIPTION
========================================================================

Decatron Bot is a free, self-service chat bot and stream automation
platform for Twitch, operated at https://twitch.decatron.net. Any
streamer can connect their channel by signing in with their Twitch
account and granting the scopes the features they enable require; there
is no invite list or approval step. The core service is free, with
optional paid supporter tiers for higher usage quotas.

The bot has been in continuous production since November 2025 and
currently serves 40 connected channels, all of them active. It runs from
a single bot account, decatronstreambot, which is present in every
connected channel.

Technically, the bot reads chat through EventSub channel.chat.message
subscriptions and sends every message through the Helix
POST /helix/chat/messages endpoint, authenticated as the bot account. It
also subscribes to channel point redemptions, follows, cheers,
subscriptions, resubscriptions, gift subscriptions, raids, hype train
events, and stream online/offline events - roughly eleven subscription
types per connected channel, which at our current size is on the order
of a few hundred active subscriptions in total.

Feature set available to broadcasters:

- Chat commands - a set of built-in commands plus per-channel custom
  commands that broadcasters create from the web dashboard.
- Timers and scheduled messages - recurring chat announcements and a
  browser-source timer overlay that reacts to channel events.
- Automated moderation - configurable banned-word filters with
  escalating warnings, timeouts, and a strike system, all controlled by
  the broadcaster.
- Event alerts - on-screen and in-chat alerts for follows,
  subscriptions, cheers, raids, and channel point redemptions,
  including sound alerts.
- Viewer engagement - watch time tracking, an experience and loyalty
  points system, giveaways managed from the dashboard, and tip goals.
- AI chat assistant - an opt-in feature that lets viewers ask the bot
  questions in chat, with per-channel limits and permissions set by the
  broadcaster.
- Text-to-speech - optional TTS that a broadcaster can trigger from a
  chat command, a channel point redemption, a cheer, or a viewer role.
- Discord integration - go-live announcements posted to a linked
  Discord server.

Every feature is opt-in and configured per channel from the web
dashboard, and any feature can be disabled there individually. Our
dashboard also has a single Bot Status switch that turns the bot off for
a channel outright: the bot leaves that chat and stops posting
immediately. Broadcasters who want to remove us entirely revoke the
application's access from their Twitch account settings.

REASON FOR BOT VERIFICATION REQUEST
(Please include the rate limit that you are hitting)
========================================================================

Current rate limit situation - stated honestly: we are not yet being
throttled. Our outgoing chat volume peaks at roughly 9 messages per
minute across all channels combined, and we have no 429 responses in our
logs. We would rather state that plainly than overstate our situation.

We are requesting verification for three reasons:

1. Our architecture concentrates all traffic on one account, and it is
scaling. Decatron Bot serves 40 channels from a single bot account, so
every message from every channel shares one rate limit budget rather
than being spread across accounts. Under the standard
20-messages-per-30-seconds limit that applies to a non-verified bot, our
send path is a single serialized queue. Today that is comfortable. Our
growth is not linear: we added 1-3 channels per month through the first
half of 2026, then 13 in July alone and 4 in the first four days of
August. Our EventSub subscription count grows by roughly eleven
subscriptions per channel we onboard. We are asking now so that we are
not requesting verification retroactively, from a position where users
are already experiencing dropped or delayed messages during peak
concurrent streams.

2. Viewer trust. The verified badge is what tells a viewer that a bot
posting in their chat is a legitimate, reviewed service rather than an
unknown account. Established chat bots such as Nightbot and
StreamElements carry it - StreamElements maintains it across multiple
accounts. For a bot that is joining an increasing number of third-party
channels, that badge is a meaningful signal to broadcasters deciding
whether to trust us with moderation permissions, and to viewers seeing
our messages.

3. Correct platform identity. We already send all chat through the
official Helix POST /helix/chat/messages endpoint with the bot as
sender_id, and we read chat through EventSub rather than IRC - the
current, recommended integration path. Verification would align our
platform identity with an integration we have deliberately built to
Twitch's current standards.

If our current volume does not meet the threshold for verification, we
understand and would welcome guidance on what usage level we should
reach before reapplying.

BOT COMMANDS
(Please share the full list with explanation of what each command does)
========================================================================

Decatron Bot uses the "!" prefix. Commands fall into six groups. Unless
stated otherwise, a command is available to everyone in chat, and every
command can be individually disabled by the broadcaster from our web
dashboard. Permission levels and cooldowns marked "configurable" are set
per channel by the broadcaster; the value shown is our default.

The bot is under active development and we add features regularly, so
this list reflects the commands in production as of August 2026.


--- STREAM TIMER / MARATHON ---

Our main feature is a countdown timer shown as a browser source overlay,
used for subathons and marathon streams, where channel events add time.

!dstart <duration>  Starts the timer with a given duration, for example
                    "!dstart 5m" or "!dstart 1h30m".
                    Moderators and broadcaster only.

!dtimer <duration>  Starts the timer, or adds and removes time from a
                    running one ("!dtimer add 1h").
                    Moderators and broadcaster only.

!dpause             Pauses the running timer.
                    Moderators and broadcaster only.

!dplay              Resumes a paused timer.
                    Moderators and broadcaster only.

!dreset             Resets the timer back to its full duration.
                    Moderators and broadcaster only.

!dstop              Stops the timer and hides the overlay.
                    Moderators and broadcaster only.

!dtiempo            Replies with the time remaining on the timer.
                    Everyone by default (configurable).
                    30 second cooldown by default (configurable).

!dcuando            Replies with the date and time the timer will end.
                    Everyone by default (configurable).
                    30 second cooldown by default (configurable).

!dstats             Replies with statistics for the current timer
                    session, such as time added and events received.
                    Everyone by default (configurable).
                    30 second cooldown by default (configurable).

!drecord            Replies with the channel's all-time longest timer
                    session.
                    Everyone by default (configurable).
                    60 second cooldown by default (configurable).

!dtop               Replies with the viewers who contributed the most
                    time to the current session.
                    Everyone by default (configurable).
                    60 second cooldown by default (configurable).


--- STREAM MANAGEMENT ---

!title              With no argument, replies with the current stream
                    title. With text after it, updates the stream title.
                    Reading is open to everyone; changing the title
                    requires moderator or broadcaster.

!t                  Short alias of !title, identical behaviour.

!game               With no argument, replies with the current stream
                    category. With text after it, updates the category.
                    Reading is open to everyone; changing the category
                    requires moderator or broadcaster.

!g                  Manages a channel's category shortcuts, with the
                    subcommands help, list, set, and remove. Lets a
                    broadcaster define a short word that switches the
                    stream to a given category.
                    Moderators and broadcaster only.

<channel shortcuts> Shortcuts created through !g. Each one switches the
                    stream category in a single word. They exist only in
                    the channel that created them.
                    Moderators and broadcaster only.


--- COMMUNITY AND VIEWER COMMANDS ---

!followage          Replies with how long the viewer has been following
                    the channel.
                    Everyone.

!watchtime          Replies with how long the viewer has been watching
                    the current stream.
                    Permission configurable per channel.
                    Separate global and per-user cooldowns, configurable.
                    Broadcasters may define their own aliases for it.

!commands           Replies with a link to the channel's public command
                    list on our website.
                    Everyone. 10 second global cooldown.

!so <username>      Posts a shoutout for another streamer, including
                    their profile information and most recent clip.
                    Moderators and broadcaster only.
                    30 second cooldown per shouted-out user.

!ia <question>      Asks our AI assistant a question and replies in chat.
                    Disabled by default; the broadcaster must enable it
                    and choose which viewer roles may use it.
                    Channel-wide cooldown, configurable, with usage
                    quotas per channel.

!tts <text>         Reads the message aloud through text-to-speech on the
                    broadcaster's stream. The command name is
                    configurable per channel; !tts is the default.
                    Allowed roles are set by the broadcaster.


--- OPTIONAL MINI-GAME (GACHA) ---

An opt-in collectible mini-game. Disabled unless the broadcaster turns
it on. Every subcommand has its own permission level and its own global
and per-user cooldowns, all configurable, and broadcasters may define
custom aliases for them.

!gacha <subcommand> Main entry point. Subcommands: pull (draw an item),
                    pulls (show remaining draws), col (show collection),
                    buy (buy draws with channel currency), price (show
                    cost), donate, pause, resume. Subcommands also accept
                    Spanish and Portuguese spellings.

!gc <subcommand>    Short alias of !gacha.

!gcpull, !gcpulls, !gccol, !gcbuy, !gcprice, !gcpause, !gcresume
                    Single-word shortcuts equivalent to the matching
                    !gacha subcommand.


--- OPTIONAL GAME TRACKER (FORTNITE) ---

!spirits            Shows the viewer's collection progress for a
                    Fortnite in-game collectible set. Also accepts
                    "!spirits top" for a channel leaderboard and
                    "!spirits missing" for items not yet collected.

!spirit <name>      Marks a collectible as obtained.
                    "!spirit remove <name>" unmarks it.


--- BROADCASTER-DEFINED CUSTOM COMMANDS ---

!crear              Lets a broadcaster or moderator create a custom
                    command for their own channel directly from chat.
                    Moderators and broadcaster only.

<custom commands>   Broadcasters can create their own commands, either
                    from chat with !crear or from our web dashboard.
                    These exist only in the channel that created them,
                    and are subject to exactly the same rate limiting as
                    every other message we send. There are currently 24
                    such commands across 6 channels. Typical examples our
                    users have created are !redes (social links), !donar
                    (a link to the streamer's donation page), and !ds (a
                    link to their Discord server).

                    Most custom commands simply reply with text the
                    broadcaster wrote, optionally including placeholders
                    such as the viewer's name, the current category, or
                    the stream uptime.

                    For broadcasters who want conditional replies, our
                    web dashboard also offers a small purpose-built
                    scripting editor. We want to be precise about what
                    this is, since "scripting" can mean very different
                    things:

                    - The language has exactly three statements: assign a
                      variable, evaluate a condition, and send a chat
                      message. It has no loops, so the number of messages
                      a command can send is fixed by the script text
                      itself and cannot grow at runtime.
                    - It offers a small closed set of helper functions:
                      simple counters and dice/random pickers.
                    - It can read a fixed, closed set of values: the
                      viewer's name, a mentioned user, the channel name,
                      the current category, and the stream uptime.
                    - It has no network access, no file system access,
                      and no ability to execute arbitrary code. It is not
                      a general-purpose programming language and cannot
                      be used to call the Twitch API directly.
                    - Scripts only ever affect the channel that owns
                      them, and are validated for syntax before they are
                      saved. The dashboard editor has a preview mode that
                      runs a script against test data without sending
                      anything to chat.

                    There are currently 20 such scripted commands in
                    production. A representative example is a !dado
                    ("dice") command that rolls a number and posts the
                    result naming the viewer who rolled it.


--- NON-COMMAND CHAT BEHAVIOUR ---

For completeness, the bot also posts messages that are not replies to a
command:

- Scheduled recurring announcements configured by the broadcaster.
- Alerts for follows, subscriptions, resubscriptions, gift
  subscriptions, cheers, raids, and hype trains, where enabled.
- Automated moderation actions: deleting messages matching a
  broadcaster-configured banned word list, issuing warnings, timeouts,
  and bans according to a strike system the broadcaster controls.
- Stream going live and going offline notices.

All of the above are opt-in per channel.

--- PANEL 4 ---
Titulo: How to remove the bot / Como quitar el bot

TURN THE BOT OFF (reversible)
Sign in at https://twitch.decatron.net, go to Settings -> Bot Status and
switch it off. The bot immediately leaves your chat and stops posting.
Turn it back on at any time.

REMOVE IT COMPLETELY
1. Go to https://www.twitch.tv/settings/connections
2. Find Decatron under "Other Connections".
3. Click "Disconnect".
This revokes our access to your channel entirely. You can also unmod or
ban the bot account from your channel at any time.
